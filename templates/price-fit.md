# Price fit

2 small bits of math. Both use your own numbers only.

## 1. The middle price

The missing step sits between 2 offers you already sell. This finds the price that's the same size of jump from both sides.

1. Price below the gap: $ ____
2. Price above the gap: $ ____
3. Multiply them: ____
4. Take the square root: ____
5. Round to a price that looks like your other prices: $ ____

Worked example:

- Below the gap: $40
- Above the gap: $1,000
- 40 x 1,000 = 40,000
- Square root of 40,000 = 200
- The missing step sits near $200. From $40 to $200 is 5 times. From $200 to $1,000 is 5 times. Neither feels like a leap.

For the technical: this is the geometric mean of the 2 prices.

### If nothing sits below the gap

If your low level is empty, there's no lower price to multiply. Don't force the math. Ask yourself: "What's a price a stranger could say yes to without thinking it over?" Write that down as your starting price.

### If nothing sits above the gap

If your high level is empty, start from what the work takes. Use the hours check below and work up from your hours.

## 2. The hours check

A price also tells you what kind of offer fits.

1. What an hour of my time is worth to me: $ ____
2. The middle price from above: $ ____
3. Divide the price by your hourly worth: ____ hours

Read the result:

- **Less than 1 hour:** it has to be something they do themselves. A guide, a template, a tool. Your time can't be in each sale.
- **A few hours:** it can be done with you. A call, a review, a short workshop.
- **Many hours:** it can be done for them, if you have the hours each week to deliver it.

4. Hours I can give to delivery each week: ____
5. At this price and type, how many sales a week could I deliver? ____

If the type that fits the price isn't what your buyers asked for, adjust one of the 2. Either shape the offer to fit the price, or move the price to fit the offer and check the jumps again.

## 3. Check the jumps again

With the new price added, list your prices in order and divide each by the one below it. No single jump should stand far above the others. If one still does, that's your next missing step.
